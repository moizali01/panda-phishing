Seasonal Flu: Why It Comes Back Every Year

By Dr. Samuel Reyes · June 28, 2026

Influenza returns each winter and sends millions to bed. Knowing how it spreads, how it differs from a common cold, and how to prevent it is worth a few minutes.

Influenza, or the flu, is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses. Unlike the common cold, the flu tends to come on suddenly and hit harder, with fever, body aches, chills, fatigue, cough, and headache.

The flu returns every year because the virus constantly changes. Small mutations mean the immunity you built last year may not fully protect you this year, which is why the vaccine is updated annually to match the strains expected to circulate.

It spreads easily through droplets when people cough, sneeze, or talk, and by touching contaminated surfaces and then the face. Crowded indoor settings in cold months create ideal conditions for outbreaks, giving the flu its seasonal pattern.

Prevention centers on the annual flu vaccine, which lowers the risk of infection and reduces severity if you do get sick. Frequent handwashing, covering coughs, and staying home when ill all help limit spread to others.

For most healthy people the flu is unpleasant but not dangerous, resolving within a week or two. For young children, older adults, pregnant people, and those with chronic conditions it can be serious. This article is general information; seek professional advice for specific concerns.
