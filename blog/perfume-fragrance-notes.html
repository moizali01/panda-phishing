Top, Heart, Base: How to Read a Fragrance Like an Expert

By Camille Rousseau · September 25, 2026

Every great perfume tells a story in three acts. Once you understand how notes unfold over time, you will never smell a fragrance the same way again.

A perfume is not a single smell but a journey that evolves on your skin over hours. Perfumers build that journey in three layers, known as the fragrance pyramid: top notes, heart notes, and base notes. Learning to recognize them is the first step from casual wearer to true enthusiast.

Top notes are your first impression, the scents you smell in the opening minutes. They are light, volatile molecules such as citrus, bergamot, and fresh herbs that evaporate quickly. They grab attention on a blotter but fade fast, so never judge a fragrance by its opening alone.

Heart notes, also called middle notes, emerge as the top notes fade and form the character of the perfume. This is where florals like rose and jasmine, spices, and green notes live. The heart typically lasts for several hours and defines what the fragrance is really about.

Base notes are the foundation that lingers longest, sometimes for a full day. Rich, heavy materials such as sandalwood, vanilla, musk, amber, and oakmoss anchor the scent and give it depth and staying power. They also slow the evaporation of the lighter notes above them.

To experience the pyramid, spray a fragrance and revisit it at fifteen minutes, one hour, and four hours. You will notice it transform at each stage. Understanding this structure helps you choose scents that work for you not just at first spritz, but all day long.
