The Greatest Goals in Football History

By Amara Boateng · July 23, 2026

A mazy solo run, an impossible volley, a goal scored from the halfway line. These are the strikes that fans replay forever.

Some goals are simply finishes. Others become legend. The greatest goals in football history combine audacity, skill, and timing in a way that fans remember for decades.

Diego Maradona's second goal against England in 1986 is often called the greatest ever scored. He collected the ball in his own half and dribbled past half the England team before slotting home, all in a matter of seconds. In the same match, his controversial 'Hand of God' goal showed the other, more cunning side of his genius.

Volleys hold a special place in the pantheon. Zinedine Zidane's left-footed strike in a Champions League final, meeting a dropping ball on the edge of the box, is a masterclass in technique and nerve under the biggest possible spotlight.

Long-range audacity thrills crowds too. Players who spot a goalkeeper off the line and dare to shoot from distance, sometimes from near the halfway line, produce moments of pure spectacle when the gamble pays off.

What unites the greatest goals is not just difficulty but context. A brilliant goal in a meaningless match is admired; the same goal in a World Cup knockout or a title decider becomes immortal. Pressure is the ingredient that turns a great goal into a historic one.
