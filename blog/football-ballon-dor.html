The Ballon d'Or: Football's Most Coveted Individual Prize

By Diego Ferreira · August 11, 2026

Awarded to the best player in the world each year, the Ballon d'Or has crowned the sport's greatest names. Two players rewrote its record books entirely.

The Ballon d'Or, French for 'Golden Ball,' has been awarded since 1956 to recognize the finest footballer in the world over a given year. Originally voted on by journalists, it has become the sport's most prestigious individual honour.

For most of its history, the award passed between a rotating cast of legends: Johan Cruyff and Michel Platini each won it three times, while greats like Franz Beckenbauer, Marco van Basten, and Ronaldo of Brazil also claimed multiple trophies.

Then came an era of unprecedented dominance. Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo traded the award back and forth for well over a decade, combining for more than a dozen titles between them. Messi's tally in particular set a record that may stand for generations.

The award has evolved over time. Originally limited to European players, it opened to players at European clubs and then to footballers worldwide, broadening the pool and better reflecting the global nature of the modern game.

Beyond the trophies, the Ballon d'Or sparks endless debate. Should it reward a single sensational season or a body of brilliance? Should team success count? Those arguments are part of its appeal, keeping football fans arguing long after the winner is announced.
