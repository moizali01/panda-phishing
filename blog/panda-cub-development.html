The Size of a Stick of Butter: How Panda Cubs Grow Up

By Priya Nandakumar · July 19, 2026

Newborn pandas are tiny, blind, and almost helpless. The journey from a 100-gram pink cub to a 100-kilogram adult is one of the most dramatic in the animal world.

A newborn giant panda weighs about 100 grams, roughly the size of a stick of butter and around one nine-hundredth of its mother's weight. That ratio is one of the most extreme of any mammal. The cub is born pink, blind, nearly hairless, and completely dependent on its mother.

For the first weeks of life the cub cannot regulate its own temperature or move around. The mother cradles it almost constantly, often not even leaving to eat. The famous black-and-white markings appear gradually over the first month as the cub's fur grows in.

Eyes open at around six to eight weeks, and the cub begins to crawl at about ten weeks. By several months of age it is walking, playing, and starting to explore, though it will continue to nurse well into its first year before bamboo becomes its main food.

In the wild, a panda mother typically raises a single cub at a time. When twins are born, she often cannot care for both, and one may not survive. In captivity, keepers sometimes swap twins back and forth with the mother so that each gets attention, a technique that has raised twin survival dramatically.

Young pandas stay with their mothers for around 18 months to two years before striking out on their own. That long period of learning, climbing, and play is essential preparation for a solitary adult life spent largely alone in the bamboo forest.
