Self-Driving Cars: How Close Are We Really?

By Grace Lindqvist · June 11, 2026

Autonomous vehicles have been 'just around the corner' for years. Understanding the levels of automation reveals how much progress has truly been made.

Self-driving cars capture the imagination like few technologies, promising safer roads and freed-up time. But the reality is more nuanced than the headlines suggest, and the path to full autonomy has proven longer and harder than many predicted.

Engineers describe autonomy in levels from zero to five. Level 0 means no automation; Levels 1 and 2 cover driver-assistance features like adaptive cruise control and lane-keeping, which are now common. The driver remains fully responsible throughout these levels.

The real leap is to Levels 3 and above, where the car can handle driving itself under certain conditions. Level 4 vehicles can drive themselves fully within defined areas, such as mapped city zones, while Level 5, true drive-anywhere autonomy, remains a distant goal.

The hardest problems are not highways but the chaos of everyday streets: unpredictable pedestrians, cyclists, bad weather, and unusual situations that human drivers handle intuitively. Teaching software to deal with endless edge cases has slowed progress considerably.

Robotaxi services now operate in limited areas, offering a glimpse of the future. But a car that can drive itself anywhere, in any conditions, without a human ready to take over, is not yet here. The revolution is arriving gradually, not all at once.
